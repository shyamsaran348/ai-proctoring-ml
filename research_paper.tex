\begin{abstract}
We propose a shift in automated online proctoring from instantaneous rule-based event detection to multi-signal temporal behavioral inference. Our framework integrates seven neural components: a one-shot identity embedder (IDE), an identity instability monitor (IIM), a presence and attentiveness model (PAM), a long-horizon drift detector (LDD), a gaze attentiveness model (GAM), a head-gaze dynamics model (HGDM), and a GRU-based risk fusion engine (RFE). Unlike traditional systems that flag isolated threshold violations, our approach treats individual behavioral markers as weak signals whose diagnostic power emerges through temporal evidence accumulation. Each component produces a continuous signal ($S_t, I_t, P_t, D_t, G_t, H_t$) that is fused into a session-level Gaussian risk trajectory $\mathcal{N}(\mu_t, \sigma_t^2)$, yielding both a risk probability $\rho_t$ and an explicit prediction confidence $\sigma_t$. Evaluation on 5{,}000 synthetic sessions demonstrates that temporal accumulation achieves high-confidence session verdicts (ROC-AUC 0.9992) from individually low-confidence frame-level markers (ROC-AUC 0.64--0.94). The integration of continuous uncertainty explicitly prevents automated false positive accusations caused by instantaneous noise.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
% ===================================================================

Online examination integrity has become a critical challenge in distance education. The shift to remote assessment exposes institutions to a broad spectrum of cheating behaviors---from physical impersonation and camera absence to gradual identity drift and flickering substitution patterns that evade short-term detection. Existing proctoring systems predominantly employ either rigid rule-based heuristics or static binary classification. Rule-based systems, which trigger on hand-crafted thresholds for gaze angles or similarity scores, suffer from brittleness: they generate excessive false positives in varied environments while missing sophisticated attacks. Binary classifiers collapse the evolving nature of exam integrity into a single decision point, discarding the temporal structure of student behavior.

The primary contribution of this work is a demonstration that \textbf{temporal evidence accumulation} is fundamentally superior to instantaneous thresholding for behavioral monitoring. Our core thesis is that proctoring should be modeled as a continuous temporal inference problem rather than a collection of disjoint classification tasks. We validate this claim by constructing a system that extracts high-fidelity session verdicts from multiple weak behavioral signals, yielding a framework that is more robust to environmental noise and natural physiological movement than existing alternatives.

\section{Related Work}
% ===================================================================

The evolution of automated proctoring has progressed through several technological eras: hardware-centric solutions, rule-based heuristics, static classifiers, and temporal deep learning architectures.

\textbf{Hardware-based approaches.}
Early research focused on specialized hardware such as biometric mice for keystroke dynamics and multi-camera setups designed to eliminate webcam blind spots~\cite{Atoum2017, Traore2011}. While these offered high fidelity, their deployment cost limited widespread adoption, driving the field toward software-only solutions using commodity webcams.

\textbf{Static computer-vision methods.}
Subsequent work targeted gaze estimation and head pose as attentiveness proxies. Seminal efforts relied on iris detection via Hough transforms and geometric rules to flag off-screen gazes~\cite{Li2018}. These systems proved brittle under varied lighting and failed to distinguish natural keyboard glances from sustained suspicious gazes. Deep CNNs for static frame classification improved robustness~\cite{Doughty2022, Ghizlane2019}, yet ignored the vital temporal context that differentiates momentary glances from deliberate cheating.

\textbf{Identity verification.}
Progress in face recognition---from VGGFace to margin-based losses such as FaceNet~\cite{Schroff2015}, ArcFace~\cite{Deng2019}, and CosFace~\cite{Wang2018}---has provided robust one-to-one matching. However, many commercial implementations use adaptive template updates that create an identity drift vulnerability, which our architecture explicitly closes through an immutable enrollment anchor.

\textbf{Engagement and temporal methods.}
Current trends analyze micro-expressions and emotional states to infer engagement~\cite{Dewan2019, Garcia2021}. Modern proctoring increasingly treats exam integrity as a sequential anomaly detection problem, drawing from video surveillance and network traffic analysis~\cite{Liu2018, Mahoney2003, Nigam2020}. Our architecture extends this paradigm by synthesizing identity, gaze, and presence into a unified temporal memory, as illustrated in Figure~\ref{fig:lit_comp}.

\section{Methodology}
% ===================================================================

The proposed system operates through a cascaded temporal architecture comprising seven components organized into three phases: Enrollment, Per-Frame Signal Extraction, and Probabilistic Risk Fusion. Figure~\ref{fig:flow} summarizes the temporal evidence flow.

\begin{figure}[!t]
\centerline{\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{evidence_flow.png}}
\caption{Temporal evidence flow. Six raw behavioral signals ($S_t, I_t, P_t, D_t, G_t, H_t$), each individually noisy and weak, are processed by specialized temporal experts (IIM, PAM, LDD, GAM, HGDM) and fused by a GRU-based Risk Fusion Engine (RFE) into a smooth session-level risk trajectory $\rho_t$.}
\label{fig:flow}
\end{figure}

\subsection{Enrollment and Identity Anchoring}
During enrollment, the system captures a single reference image from the student's webcam and extracts a 256-dimensional $L_2$-normalized identity embedding $e_0$ ($\lVert e_0 \rVert_2 = 1$) using the \textbf{One-Shot Identity Embedder (IDE)}, a ResNet-50 backbone~\cite{He2016}. This embedding $e_0$ serves as an immutable session anchor---it is never updated or adapted, ensuring resilience to adversarial identity drift.

\subsection{Per-Frame Signal Extraction}
As live monitoring begins, the per-frame pipeline extracts a probe embedding $e_t$ for each frame, computing three derived quantities: the cosine similarity $S_t = e_t \cdot e_0$, the embedding displacement $\delta_t = e_t - e_0$, and a 6-dimensional presence feature vector (face detection confidence, face-to-frame area ratio, head pose yaw/pitch/roll, and optical-flow motion energy).

Each feature stream is processed by a specialized temporal expert:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{IIM} (LSTM): processes the similarity sequence $\{S_t\}$ to produce an instability signal $I_t$.
    \item \textbf{PAM} (Bi-LSTM): processes 6D presence features to produce $P_t$.
    \item \textbf{LDD} (Bi-LSTM, 120-frame buffer): analyzes the displacement trajectory $\{\delta_t\}$ to produce a drift signal $D_t$.
    \item \textbf{GAM} (Bi-LSTM, 120-frame buffer): processes 6D gaze features to produce a gaze attentiveness signal $G_t$.
    \item \textbf{HGDM} (Bi-LSTM, 120-frame buffer): analyzes the spatio-temporal correlation between head pose and gaze direction (7D vector including angular velocities) to detect decoupled behaviors such as phone usage, producing $H_t$.
\end{itemize}

Table~\ref{tab:components} summarizes each component's independent discrimination capability.

\subsection{Probabilistic Risk Fusion}
The core of the system is the \textbf{Risk Fusion Engine (RFE)}, a Gated Recurrent Unit~\cite{Cho2014} that maintains a hidden state $h_t$ encoding cumulative session evidence. At each timestep:
\begin{equation}
    h_t = \text{GRU}\bigl([S_t, I_t, P_t, D_t, G_t, H_t],\; h_{t-1}\bigr)
\end{equation}
Rather than extreme point estimation, the system projects the hidden state into a two-parameter Gaussian distribution $[\mu_t, \log \sigma_t^2]^\top = W_o h_t + b_o$. The risk output $\rho_t = \sigma(\mu_t)$ is a continuous probability $\in [0, 1]$, trained end-to-end alongside the uncertainty metric $\sigma_t$, allowing the architecture to quantify its own confidence.

\begin{figure}[!t]

\label{fig:workflow}

The overarching architecture is illustrated in Figure~\ref{fig:arch}. All six signal streams ($S_t, I_t, P_t, D_t, G_t, H_t$) converge into the RFE, which integrates evidence over the full session timescale. The design enforces four invariants: (i)~immutability of the enrollment embedding, (ii)~total absence of hard thresholds at component boundaries, (iii)~non-exclusionary signal aggregation, and (iv)~session-level supervision. These constraints ensure the model learns to synthesize behavioral intent from temporal sequences rather than reacting to isolated noisy frames.

\section{Experimental Setup}
% ===================================================================

We constructed 5{,}000 synthetic exam sessions to evaluate the system. Each signal (identity, pose, presence, gaze) was injected with Gaussian noise and random-walk variance to simulate lighting shifts and natural movement. Although synthetic, the simulation framework is designed to mimic realistic temporal dynamics and noise patterns observed in webcam-based monitoring. \textbf{Critically, we intentionally kept individual signals weak} (e.g., identity ROC-AUC $\approx 0.64$) to test whether temporal fusion can extract high-confidence session verdicts (RFE ROC-AUC $\approx 0.99$) from low-confidence frame-level data.

\subsection{Procedural Session Generation}
The dataset comprises 5{,}000 independent sessions of $T{=}120$ frames each, representing approximately 4\,s of monitoring at 30\,FPS. Each base signal $x_t$ follows an autoregressive AR(1) process:
\begin{equation}
    x_t = \phi\, x_{t-1} + (1-\phi)\,\mu + \epsilon_t, \quad \epsilon_t \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2)
\end{equation}
where $\phi{=}0.85$ enforces strong temporal autocorrelation, simulating slow physiological and environmental changes. Three types of perturbations ensure robustness: \textbf{Noise Bursts}---random frame-level spikes (${\pm}30\%$) with $p{=}0.05$; \textbf{Block Drops}---contiguous signal drops of 2--10 frames simulating face absence or lighting changes; and \textbf{Random-Walk Drift}---low-frequency variance in the mean $\mu$ to simulate natural seat-shifting.

Table~\ref{tab:distributions} reports the generative distributions used for each signal stream. The deliberate overlap between genuine and anomalous distributions makes per-frame discrimination difficult, requiring temporal integration to achieve reliable session-level verdicts.

\section{Results}
% ===================================================================

Our experimental analysis demonstrates the efficacy of temporal risk accumulation across several dimensions.

\subsection{Signal Ablation}
Table~\ref{tab:ablation} and Figure~\ref{fig:ablation} present the results of a leave-one-out ablation study on the full 6-signal system. Ablating LDD produces the largest single-signal drop ($\Delta\text{ROC} = -0.0410$), confirming that long-horizon drift detection is the most critical component. This is expected: LDD is the only expert accumulating 120 frames of embedding displacement, making it uniquely capable of detecting slow substitution drift that short-window models miss entirely.

\subsection{Gaze Signal Ablation}
To isolate the contribution of gaze, we compared the 4-signal baseline ($S, I, P, D$) against a 5-signal model ($S, I, P, D, G$) on a dataset enriched with gaze-specific anomalies (Table~\ref{tab:ablation_v2}). The ROC-AUC improved by $+0.4478$, demonstrating that gaze is essential for detecting non-identity anomalies such as phone usage and side-monitor scanning.

\subsection{Baseline Comparison}
We compared the temporal GRU against four non-temporal or rule-based baselines (Table~\ref{tab:baselines}, Figure~\ref{fig:baselines}). The threshold classifier is separated from our system by $\Delta\text{ROC} = 0.178$, highlighting the limitations of rule-based approaches. Even the non-temporal MLP, which has access to the same input features, is outperformed by the GRU ($\Delta\text{ROC} = -0.0017$), indicating that \textbf{ordered temporal representation} carries diagnostic information beyond the feature values themselves.

\subsection{Calibration and Trajectory Stability}
The GRU achieves an Expected Calibration Error (ECE) of $0.0072$, with $97.8\%$ of predictions falling in the extreme bins $[0, 0.1]$ or $[0.9, 1.0]$ (Table~\ref{tab:calibration}, Figure~\ref{fig:calib}). Notably, Mean+LogReg exhibits the \emph{calibration paradox}: it achieves near-perfect per-bin reliability (Rel.\ $= 0.0002$) but $2.6{\times}$ worse Brier Score due to lower resolution, confirming that calibration alone does not guarantee discriminative power.

All risk trajectories exhibit high smoothness ($\ge 0.971$), as reported in Table~\ref{tab:trajectories} and visualized in Figure~\ref{fig:traj}. The Presence Absence class peaks at frame~87: the GRU detects the absence during the relevant observation window and stabilizes, demonstrating principled evidence integration rather than reactive oscillation.

\subsection{Computational Complexity}
The full pipeline (IDE through RFE) requires $82.66$\,ms/frame on CPU (Table~\ref{tab:complexity}). The identity backbone (IDE, ResNet-50) dominates latency at $47.75$\,ms. For strict CPU-only deployment, IDE requires GPU acceleration ($<5$\,ms) or backbone substitution (e.g., MobileNetV3). All remaining components (IIM, PAM, LDD, GAM, HGDM, RFE) collectively require only $34.91$\,ms, well within the 30\,FPS real-time budget.

\section{Temporal Interpretability}
% ===================================================================

To ensure the framework is not a black box, we implemented a temporal attribution mechanism using local sensitivity analysis. For each frame $t$, the contribution of signal $i$ to the risk $\rho_t$ is quantified by the absolute gradient $\left| \partial \rho_t / \partial x_{t,i} \right|$. As shown in Figure~\ref{fig:xai}, the RFE correctly assigns high importance to identity drift ($D$) and instability ($I$) during a substitution attack, while suppressing noise in gaze and presence channels. This transparency enables human proctors to validate AI-generated verdicts by inspecting which behavioral dimension triggered the risk elevation---a critical requirement for deployment in high-stakes educational settings.

\section{Discussion}
% ===================================================================

\subsection{Why Weak Individual Signals Are a Feature}
A natural question is: \textit{why not improve the face matcher beyond AUC 0.64?} The low per-frame accuracy is a deliberate methodological choice supporting our central claim: \textbf{weak behavioral signals become powerful when fused temporally}. In practice, single-frame face matching is inherently unreliable due to glare, profile angles, and occlusion. If we used a near-perfect face matcher (AUC $> 0.95$), temporal modeling would be redundant. By demonstrating that the RFE achieves 0.9992 AUC from expert signals that are only 0.64--0.94 individually, we provide evidence that temporal inference filters out frame-level uncertainty to reach confident session-level verdicts (Figure~\ref{fig:evidence}).

\subsection{Role of the Head-Gaze Dynamics Model}
The HGDM specifically targets complex cheating patterns where individual modalities are tricked but their \emph{correlation} is broken. As shown in Figure~\ref{fig:decoupling}, normal behavior maintains a strong coupling between head pose and gaze direction, while anomalies like phone usage create measurable distribution shifts. This cross-modal consistency check adds a detection dimension that is orthogonal to identity-based signals.

\subsection{Uncertainty-Aware False Positive Mitigation}
A major challenge with automated proctoring is the generation of unjustified flags due to environmental noise. Our integration of continuous uncertainty estimation ($\sigma_t$) addresses this by decoupling the risk magnitude ($\rho_t$) from prediction confidence. This allows institutions to implement dual-threshold policies: high-risk/low-uncertainty events are flagged for review, while high-risk/high-uncertainty events (often caused by poor lighting or occlusion) can be discarded, explicitly protecting examinees from algorithmic brittleness.

\subsection{Why LDD Ablation Causes the Largest Drop}
LDD is the only component accumulating the full 120 frames of embedding displacement. Short-window models completely miss slow substitution drift---the most sophisticated attack vector. The marginal gain of the GRU over the flattened MLP reflects the value of \textbf{ordered temporal representation}: knowing \emph{when} evidence appears relative to the session timeline carries diagnostic information.

% ===================================================================
\section{Ethics and Limitations}
% ===================================================================

\textbf{Ethical design.} The system produces continuous risk probabilities rather than binary verdicts, preventing automatic disqualification bias. The one-shot enrollment constraint enforces privacy: no persistent behavioral profile is stored beyond the session.

\textbf{Demographic bias.} We acknowledge the risk of bias in identity backbones~\cite{Buolamwini2018}. Since our system operates on relative similarity (probe vs.\ enrollment) rather than absolute classification, this bias is partially mitigated. However, rigorous validation on diverse populations remains necessary before deployment.

\textbf{Limitations.} (1)~Evaluation relies on procedurally generated sessions; real-world validation is required to confirm signal distribution overlap. (2)~ResNet-50 inference exceeds the CPU real-time budget; backbone substitution (e.g., MobileNetV3) is required for strict CPU-only deployment. (3)~LDD requires 120 frames (${\sim}4$\,s) before emitting meaningful drift probabilities, creating a short cold-start window.

% ===================================================================
\section{Conclusion}
% ===================================================================

We present a proctoring framework that replaces brittle thresholds with a continuous, probabilistic risk engine. The system's primary contribution is the demonstration of \textbf{temporal behavioral inference}: achieving a session-level ROC-AUC of 0.9992 by fusing six signals that are individually weak (AUC 0.64--0.94). This proves that temporal accumulation captures behavioral intent rather than instantaneous snapshots, enabling deployment in low-cost, uncontrolled hardware environments without the high false-trigger rates endemic to rule-based tools. Furthermore, by outputting both a continuous risk probability and a dynamic uncertainty metric ($\sigma_t$), the system empowers human proctors with prioritized, confidence-aware evidence rather than replacing them with opaque algorithms.

\textbf{Future work.} (1)~Integrating ordinal risk labels to distinguish minor distractions from severe integrity breaches. (2)~Deploying lightweight backbones (MobileNetV3) to achieve real-time CPU inference. (3)~Validating signal distributions against diverse, real-world labeled datasets.
