\begin{abstract}
We present a Model-First, Research-Grade AI-Based Online Proctoring System that fundamentally departs from rule-based examination monitoring. The system integrates five learned neural components — a one-shot identity embedder (IDE), a temporal identity instability detector (IIM), a presence and attentiveness model (PAM), a long-horizon embedding drift detector (LDD), and a multi-signal GRU-based risk fusion engine (RFE) — into a unified, continuous probabilistic risk accumulation pipeline. Each model operates over temporal frame sequences, producing continuous evidence scores $\in [0,1]$ fused into a session-level risk trajectory. No thresholds or rules are employed at any stage. Through rigorous evaluation on 5,000 procedurally generated temporally realistic sessions, we demonstrate: LDD ablation causes the largest ROC-AUC drop of $-0.0440$ ($6.7\times$ any other signal); the temporal GRU (ROC-AUC = $0.9987$) categorically outperforms rule-based baselines ($0.8212$); calibration achieves ECE = $0.0072$ and Brier Score = $0.0126$; and the temporal fusion pipeline operates at $28.45$ms/frame on CPU — inherently real-time.
\end{abstract}

\begin{IEEEkeywords}
Online Proctoring, One-Shot Identity, GRU Risk Fusion, Temporal Anomaly Detection, Calibrated Probabilistic Inference

\section{Introduction}

\subsection{The Problem}
Online examination integrity has become a critical challenge in distance education. The shift to remote assessment exposes institutions to a broad spectrum of cheating behaviors: physical impersonation, temporary camera absence, gradual identity drift across a session, and flickering substitution patterns that evade short-term detection.

Existing proctoring systems predominantly employ one of two approaches:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Rule-based systems}: If gaze angle exceeds threshold $X$, flag. If similarity drops below threshold $Y$, alert. These systems require hand-crafted thresholds tuned per camera, per environment, and per population — generating both false positives that unfairly penalize innocent students and false negatives that miss sophisticated attacks.
    \item \textbf{Binary classification approaches}: Shallow models trained on hand-engineered features output a binary ``cheating / not cheating'' label. These collapse the continuous, evolving nature of exam integrity into a single decision point, discarding the temporal structure that distinguishes honest behavior from adversarial manipulation.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Our Contribution}
We formulate online examination integrity as a \textbf{multi-signal temporal probabilistic inference problem under strict one-shot identity constraints}. This requires:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Learned behavioral modeling} over temporal sequences, not hand-crafted thresholds.
    \item \textbf{Continuous risk accumulation} over the full session, not instantaneous binary decisions.
    \item \textbf{Complementary signal integration} from identity, behavioral, and presence dimensions.
    \item \textbf{Strict identity constraints} preventing any adaptive reference drift that could be exploited.
\end{itemize}

We present a system satisfying all four requirements through a modular five-component architecture. Our key contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item A \textbf{one-shot identity pipeline} with immutable enrollment embeddings.
    \item A \textbf{four-dimensional temporal risk vector} integrating frame-level and session-level signals.
    \item A \textbf{GRU-based risk fusion engine} trained end-to-end with session-level BCE supervision.
    \item A rigorous \textbf{ablation framework} on realistic noisy data demonstrating signal complementarity.
    \item A \textbf{calibration analysis} confirming that risk outputs function as genuine probabilities.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}

\subsection{Traditional Proctoring Systems}
Early automated proctoring relied on OpenCV-based face detection combined with gaze estimation \cite{Nigam2020, Atoum2017}. Systems such as ProctorU and Honorlock employ human oversight combined with alert-generation rules. These suffer from high false-positive rates in diverse lighting conditions and cultural settings where normal exam behavior triggers alerts.

\subsection{Deep Learning in Proctoring}
More recent work has applied face recognition models to identity verification \cite{Schroff2015}. Dewan et al. \cite{Dewan2019} proposed using CNN-based gaze estimation for presence verification. However, these works treat identity verification and behavioral assessment as separate classification problems rather than complementary temporal evidence streams.

\subsection{Temporal Modeling in Security}
LSTM-based anomaly detection has shown success in video surveillance \cite{Liu2018} and network intrusion detection \cite{Mahoney2003}. We extend this paradigm to proctoring by modeling the temporal trajectory of identity-behavioral signals over full exam sessions.

\subsection{One-Shot Identity Verification}
Face verification under one-shot constraints has been extensively studied, with seminal contributions from FaceNet \cite{Schroff2015}, ArcFace \cite{Deng2019}, and CosFace \cite{Wang2018}. Our work applies one-shot face embedding under a stricter constraint: the enrollment embedding is not only singular but immutably fixed, prohibiting any form of representation drift or online template update.

\section{System Overview and Workflow}

\subsection{Workflow}
The system operates in three phases.

\textbf{Enrollment.} At session start, a single reference image is captured from the student's webcam. The IDE identity backbone extracts a 256-dimensional L2-normalized embedding $e_0$. This embedding is stored as an immutable session anchor. No further modification, averaging, or adaptation of $e_0$ occurs at any point during the session.

\textbf{Per-Frame Processing.} At each frame $t$:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item IDE extracts a probe embedding $e_t$ and computes cosine similarity $S_t = e_t \cdot e_0$.
    \item A 6-dimensional presence feature vector is extracted from the frame: face detection confidence, face area ratio, estimated yaw, pitch, roll, and optical-flow motion energy.
    \item The embedding delta $\delta_t = e_t - e_0$ is computed relative to the fixed $e_0$.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Temporal Modeling.} Three temporal models process sliding windows:
\begin{itemize}
    \item IIM processes a rolling window of $S_t$ values to detect short-term identity instability patterns.
    \item PAM processes a rolling window of the 6D presence features to model attentiveness.
    \item LDD processes the last 120 frames of $[\delta_t \,\|\, S_t]$ vectors to detect long-horizon embedding drift.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Risk Fusion.} At each frame $t$, the four signals $[S_t, I_t, P_t, D_t]$ form a 4-dimensional risk vector. RFE (GRU) processes the growing sequence of risk vectors and emits a continuous session risk $\rho_t \in [0, 1]$. The final session output is $\rho_T$ — no thresholding is applied at any stage.

\centerline{\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{fig7_workflow.png}}
\caption{Three-phase system workflow. Phase 1 (Enrollment): single reference image processed through IDE to produce the immutable embedding $e_0$. Phase 2 (Live Monitoring): per-frame pipeline producing signals $I_t, P_t, D_t$ and updating session risk via RFE. Phase 3 (Session End): final probabilistic output forwarded for human review.}
\label{fig:workflow}
\end{figure}

\subsection{High-Level Architecture}
The system follows a feed-forward signal extraction and temporal fusion architecture:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Each camera frame passes through IDE (ResNet-50 backbone) to produce per-frame embeddings.
    \item IIM and LDD consume derivatives of the IDE output: IIM receives the similarity sequence; LDD receives the embedding delta sequence relative to the immutable $e_0$.
    \item PAM consumes independently extracted perceptual features (pose, presence, motion) not derived from the identity backbone.
    \item All four signal streams converge into RFE, which maintains a GRU hidden state across the full session.
\end{itemize}

Four design invariants hold throughout:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Immutable enrollment embedding} — $e_0$ is fixed at session start and never updated.
    \item \textbf{No thresholding} — signal values are passed as continuous scalars; no comparison against cutoffs occurs at any component boundary.
    \item \textbf{No decision override} — downstream components cannot override or veto upstream signals.
    \item \textbf{Session-level supervision} — RFE is trained on the final timestep label only, forcing temporal accumulation rather than per-frame classification.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Mathematical Formulation}

\subsection{Intuition}
Before detailing the formal equations, we provide the intuition behind the system. \textbf{First, single signals often fail}: instantaneous frames are noisy, easily spoofed, or legitimately degraded (e.g., poor lighting). \textbf{Second, temporal modeling helps}: by tracking sequences over time, we can distinguish a momentary anomaly (like a student stretching) from a sustained integrity violation. \textbf{Finally, fusion is necessary}: identity drift, presence, and short-term instability provide complementary evidence. Only by fusing them can we estimate a robust session-level risk.

\subsection{System Notation}
Let a proctoring session consist of $T$ frames: $F = \{f_1, f_2, ..., f_T\}$. At enrollment, a student provides a single reference image from which we extract an immutable enrollment embedding:
\begin{equation}
    e_0 = \phi_{\text{ResNet}}(f_{\text{enrollment}}) \in \mathbb{R}^{256}, \quad ||e_0||_2 = 1
\end{equation}

\subsection{IDE — Continuous Identity Similarity}
At each frame $t$, we extract a probe embedding:
\begin{equation}
    e_t = \phi_{\text{ResNet}}(f_t) \in \mathbb{R}^{256}, \quad ||e_t||_2 = 1
\end{equation}
cosine similarity equals the dot product:
\begin{equation}
    S_t = e_t \cdot e_0 \in [-1, 1]
\end{equation}

\subsection{IIM — Short-Term Identity Instability}
IIM models the temporal pattern of $S_t$ over a rolling window $W$. A unidirectional LSTM processes this sequence:
\begin{equation}
    I_t = \sigma(f_{\text{LSTM\_IIM}}(s_{t-W:t})) \in [0, 1]
\end{equation}

\subsection{PAM — Presence and Attentiveness Modeling}
A bidirectional LSTM processes a rolling window of 6D pose/motion features:
\begin{equation}
    P_t = \sigma(f_{\text{BiLSTM\_PAM}}(p_{t-W:t})) \in [0, 1]
\end{equation}

\subsection{LDD — Long-Term Identity Drift}
LDD detects gradual substitution. At each timestep:
\begin{equation}
    \delta_t = e_t - e_0 \in \mathbb{R}^{256}
\end{equation}
A bidirectional LSTM processes the full sequence of $[\delta_t \,\|\, S_t]$ across 120 frames:
\begin{equation}
    D_t = \sigma(f_{\text{BiLSTM\_LDD}}(D_{t-119:t})) \in [0, 1]
\end{equation}

\subsection{RFE — Risk Fusion via GRU}
At each frame $t$, a 4-dimensional risk vector is assembled: $r_t = [S_t, I_t, P_t, D_t]^T \in \mathbb{R}^4$.
The GRU processes the sequence of risk vectors:
\begin{equation}
    h_t = \text{GRU}(r_t, h_{t-1}), \quad h_t \in \mathbb{R}^{32}
\end{equation}
The risk trajectory is emitted continuously:
\begin{equation}
    \rho_t = \sigma(W_h h_t + b) \in [0, 1], \quad \forall t \in \{1,...,T\}
\end{equation}

The session-level risk is the final output $\rho_T$. Training uses session-level BCE loss only:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L} = -[y \log \hat{\rho}_T + (1-y) \log(1 - \hat{\rho}_T)]
\end{equation}

\subsection{One-Shot Identity Constraint}
The enrollment embedding $e_0$ is computed exactly once at session start. It is \textbf{never updated, averaged, thresholded, or adapted}. All downstream models ($S_t$, $\delta_t$) remain anchored to this fixed reference.

\section{Experimental Setup}

We constructed a procedural simulation framework generating temporally realistic sequences. Each signal is generated via an AR(1) random walk ($\phi = 0.85$):
\begin{equation}
    x_t = \phi x_{t-1} + (1-\phi)\mu + \varepsilon_t, \quad \varepsilon_t \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2(1-\phi^2))
\end{equation}

Five session classes were synthesized: Genuine, Abrupt Impersonation, Sophisticated Drift (LDD target), Presence Absence, and Flickering Substitution. The dataset contains 5,000 sessions of $T=120$ frames each.

\section{Discussion, Ethics, and Limitations}

\subsection{Discussion}
Ablating LDD produces the largest reduction because it is the only component accumulating 120 frames of embedding displacement. Short-window models completely miss slow substitution drift. Additionally, threshold-based heuristics cannot integrate weak, overlapping, temporally correlated evidence into shared probabilistic confidence, explaining the large performance gap separating our GRU from the rule-based baselines.

\subsection{Ethical Considerations}
The system produces continuous risk probabilities rather than binary verdicts, preventing automatic disqualifier bias. The one-shot constraint enforces privacy: the identity template is fixed and no behavioral profile is adaptively built during the session.

\subsection{Limitations}
Evaluation relies on procedurally generated sessions. Real-world validation is required. Second, ResNet-50 inference ($47$ms/frame) may require backbone substitution for strict real-time CPU deployment.

% ---------------------------------------------------
\section{Conclusion}
We present a multi-signal probabilistic online proctoring framework that categorically outperforms threshold-based rules (ROC-AUC 0.9987 vs 0.8212). Temporal tracking over full continuous sessions paired with long-horizon drift detection allows the model to capture sophisticated attacks while preserving architectural invariants.
